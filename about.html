Tarot Reading

About tarot

A tarot deck has 78 cards. Played as a game it is older than tarot reading; read as an oracle it is a deck of pictures to talk about, and this page is about the second use.

The deck

Major Arcana
22 cards, from 0 The Fool to 21 The World: the big archetypes and turning points of a life.
Minor Arcana
56 cards in four suits, Wands, Cups, Swords and Pentacles, each running Ace to Ten.
Court cards
Page, Knight, Queen and King in each suit, often read as people or ways of acting.

A reading is a spread: cards drawn into set positions, where each position asks its own question. The Celtic Cross used here has ten cards, six laid in a cross and four in a column beside it, and the last card is usually read as the likely outcome. Position names vary a little between authors.

Most modern readers treat the cards as prompts for reflection, a way to look at a question from angles you had not tried, rather than as a prediction of fixed events.

A short history

  • 1440sThe earliest known tarot decks are painted for noble families in northern Italy, such as the Visconti-Sforza decks of Milan, for a trick-taking game called trionfi, later tarocchi.
  • 1781Antoine Court de Gébelin claims the tarot preserves ancient Egyptian wisdom. There is no evidence for it, but the idea sticks.
  • Mid 1780sEtteilla (Jean-Baptiste Alliette) publishes the first deck designed for divination.
  • Late 1800sThe Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn ties the cards to astrology, the Kabbalah and the elements.
  • 1909The Rider-Waite-Smith deck appears in London: designed by Arthur Edward Waite and painted by Pamela Colman Smith, with a scene on every card, including the numbered suit cards.
  • 1910Waite’s Pictorial Key to the Tarot describes the ten-card “Ancient Celtic Method of Divination”, now known as the Celtic Cross.
